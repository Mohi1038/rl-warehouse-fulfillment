01 / 05

A 21 × 15 warehouse.

Shelves are obstacles you pick from the aisle beside them. Three docks sit on the near wall, a charger in the far corner. One tick is one cell of travel; a shift lasts 720 ticks.

02 / 05

Demand arrives in waves.

Each glowing orb is an order item dropping onto a hot shelf. Express is worth about 3× bulk; rare VIP orders are worth 5× with tight deadlines.

03 / 05

Trucks don’t wait.

Every dock has a truck leaving every 120–180 ticks; it glows red as it is about to go. The real departure is jittered and hidden from the agent. Miss it and an express order is lost; others ship later at half value.

04 / 05

One robot, one decision at a time.

It carries six weight units, drains battery as it moves — its light dims as charge falls — and must always be able to reach the charger. It is asked what to do only when it becomes free.

05 / 05

Scroll to run the clock.

The trained policy on held-out seed 100026 — a typical shift, not a hand-picked one. It ends with 65.9% of the revenue captured, against 56.7% for the Greedy rule on the same seed.

03 · Observation

What the agent sees.

At a real decision in this shift, these are the orders in the robot’s 16 slots: value, tier, dock, slack until the truck, items left, distance, whether it fits, and age.

03 · Tokens

Orders become a set.

Each order becomes a 14-feature token. A Transformer encoder treats them as a set — no order is first — so the same policy runs unchanged with 24 or 32 slots.

03 · Attention

Attention decides what matters.

A learned [CLS] token attends over every order. Beam width is the real attention weight of the trained policy at this decision; across all decisions express orders get 2× their uniform share.

03 · Action

A pointer picks one.

A pointer head scores each order slot; a small head scores DELIVER, CHARGE and WAIT. A mask removes anything invalid or unsafe. The highlighted order is the one the policy actually chose.

(04)Learning5 seeds · 2M decisions each

How far ahead should the agent care?

Actions take different amounts of time, so the textbook choice discounts by γτ per tick. With γ = 0.995 that fades within a few dozen decisions — 26 on this shift. Discounting per decision keeps the whole shift in view, and trained far better.

06 · Imagine

Before acting, imagine 32 futures.

At each decision the planner tries every valid action on 32 sampled futures — new arrivals, new truck jitter — and finishes each with the policy. It never sees the real future.

06 · Score

Every branch gets a score.

Ghost paths are the candidate actions; the bars are the revenue each one reached by the end of the shift, averaged over the sampled futures.

06 · Overrule

Sometimes it overrules PPO.

Across 100 held-out shifts this lifts revenue from 66.4% to 71.0% (paired +4.6 ± 1.7) for the same checkpoint, at about 3 s per decision.

One shared brain, three robots.

(09)LimitsWhat this is — and isn’t

Honest limits.

  1. i.

    A simulator

    Demand, layouts and reward weights are modelled assumptions, not measurements from a live facility.

  2. ii.

    The future is the real limit

    With Greedy finishing the simulations, look-ahead given the true future reaches 72.0% on the first 20 seeds, against 63.1% with sampled futures.

  3. iii.

    Planning costs compute

    Look-ahead takes about 3 s per decision; the policy alone decides in milliseconds.

  4. iv.

    Fleet coordination

    The three-robot result uses a shared policy with order claiming — evidence of scale, not optimal multi-agent coordination.
